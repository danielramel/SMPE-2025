## Univariate Descriptive Statistics

### Histograms - Handle with Care
- Binning can be tricky
- Count & bin width = huge effects on interpretation
- Stack Exchange example was mind-blowing

### Why Means Can Be Misleading
- Outliers mess everything up
- Percentages: +20% then -20% does not equal back to original
- Average speed calculations get weird
- Classic example: "The average human has 1 testicle and 1 breast"

### The Three Main Measures
Use mean, median, and mode together for better picture

### Other Important Measures:
- Variance & Standard Deviation
- Quartiles (25th-75th percentile) for boxplots
- Skewness & Kurtosis (shape of distribution)

## Simpson's Paradox

Different conclusions when data is:
- Aggregated vs Disaggregated
- Classic example: UC Berkeley gender bias case



## Correlation vs Causation

### Types of Causal Relationships:
Valid causation:
- A causes B (direct)
- A causes B and B causes A (bidirectional causation) 
- A causes B and B causes C (indirect causation)

Not actually causation:
- B causes A (reverse causation)
- A and B are both caused by C (confounding variable - C causes both)
- No causal relationship (spurious correlation)

## Experimental vs Observational Data

### Flow:
1. First: Form hypothesis 
2. Then: Design experiment to test it

### Key Point:
- Can derive hypothesis from observational data
- But need controlled experiment to establish causation

### Research Quality:
- Replicate increases reliability
- Randomize reduces bias
